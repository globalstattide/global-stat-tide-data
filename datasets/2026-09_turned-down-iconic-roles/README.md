# Actors Who Turned Down Iconic Roles

**Video:** [Actors Who Turned Down Iconic Roles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a7WnBcA02ao), published 6 September 2026
**Rows:** 116 casting stories, from Warren Beatty passing on *Kill Bill* to Drew Barrymore choosing a smaller part in *Scream*, in the order they appear in the video

> **Not every story is a simple "no".** Hollywood casting stories are often retold until they turn into legend. The **Story Status** column says how solid each one is, and four of them are myths or have been denied.

## By the numbers
- **Story status:** 54 Confirmed · 33 Reported · 20 Considered, not cast · 4 Disputed (Sylvester Stallone, Ronald Reagan, John Cusack, Kevin Bacon) · 5 Unconfirmed.
- **25 replacements won an Oscar or a Golden Globe** for the role they took, and **12** of them won an Oscar. Among them: Tom Hanks (*Forrest Gump*), Jodie Foster (*The Silence of the Lambs*), Kathy Bates (*Misery*) and Halle Berry (*Monster's Ball*).
- **Most turned-down roles:** James Bond (6 cards) and Superman (5). Benjamin Braddock in *The Graduate* appears 4 times.
- **Repeat names:** Michelle Pfeiffer, Will Smith and Josh Brolin appear 3 times each. Harrison Ford is the replacement on 3 cards (Han Solo twice, Indiana Jones), and he turned down Dr. Alan Grant in *Jurassic Park* himself.

## Sources
Casting stories come from actor and filmmaker interviews and from press reports, compiled by Global Stat Tide. The riskiest entries were checked against news interviews and Wikipedia film articles, including Fox News (Burt Ward), Slashfilm and Deadline (Tom Hanks), The Hollywood Reporter via HuffPost (*Breaking Bad*), Far Out (Jodie Foster, Mel Gibson), NME (Emma Watson) and Complex (Lizzo). Award details follow Academy Awards and Golden Globe records. Each sourced row links its page in the Source column.

## Corrections
- **Meryl Streep, *King Kong*:** listed as "turned down" in our working file. In fact Streep auditioned and was passed over: she says producer Dino De Laurentiis dismissed her looks. The voiceover tells it correctly. Status: *Considered, not cast*.
- **Gwyneth Paltrow, *Titanic*:** Paltrow says she was "one of the last two" in contention before Kate Winslet was cast, not that she turned the role down (Biography.com). Wikipedia's *Titanic* article still says she chose *Great Expectations* instead; we follow her own account. Status: *Considered, not cast*.
- **Sarah Michelle Gellar, *Clueless*:** the card shows Cher Horowitz. Wikipedia and Mental Floss both say Gellar was offered **Amber Mariens** and could not take it because *All My Children* would not release her. The table now shows Amber (played by Elisa Donovan). Status: *Confirmed*.
- **Five stories changed to *Unconfirmed*** because we found no reputable source: Montgomery Clift (*On the Waterfront*), Josh Brolin as Lex Luthor (*Batman v Superman*; reports concern Batman), Madonna as Nomi Malone (*Showgirls*; Wikipedia says she was considered for Cristal Connors), Tom Selleck as the Terminator (described only as a rumour) and Rosamund Pike as Rita Skeeter.
- **Tom Hanks, *Jerry Maguire*:** the reason in our working file was a quote we could not source. Cameron Crowe says Hanks was torn because he wanted to make his own film, *That Thing You Do!*
- **Three cards show a wrong year:** *American Beauty* card shows 2003 (correct: **1999**); *Jurassic World* card shows 2105 (correct: **2015**); *Superman* (Paul Newman card) shows 1987 (correct: **1978**). The table uses the correct years.

## Notes
- The video and this table include stories where the actor wanted the role but was blocked, such as Burt Ward (*The Graduate*) and Tom Selleck (*Raiders of the Lost Ark*), both held by TV contracts.
- **Eddie Murphy, *Ghostbusters*:** Dan Aykroyd says he wrote Winston for Murphy; director Ivan Reitman has denied this.
- **John Cusack, *Breaking Bad*:** Wikipedia's Walter White article says AMC approached Cusack and Matthew Broderick; Cusack says he was never offered it.
- **Game of Thrones, Lyanna Mormont:** the year is the series premiere (2011). The character first appeared in 2016.
