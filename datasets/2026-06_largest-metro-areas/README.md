# Tokyo vs Delhi: The 200 Largest Metro Areas, and How the UN Redrew the Map

**Video:** [Tokyo vs. Delhi: The 200 Largest Metro Areas in the World (2026)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6MR7VsflMSA) · published 28 June 2026
**Rows:** 200 cities, ranked by metro-area population as shown in the video

## Why there are two population columns
The video uses the long-standing UN estimates (World Urbanization Prospects 2018 revision), in which **Tokyo is the world's largest city**.
In **November 2025** the UN published *World Urbanization Prospects 2025* with a new, consistent way of drawing city boundaries. Under that method **Jakarta (≈42 million) is No. 1, Dhaka No. 2 and Tokyo No. 3**, and many cities move up or down.
This dataset puts both side by side so you can see the change.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Population (as shown in video) | Metro estimate used in the video |
| UN 2025 Population / UN 2025 Rank | The UN's new figure and rank for the same city |
| Rank Change | Our calculation: UN 2025 rank minus video rank (negative = higher under the new method) |
| Yearly Change, Land Area, Density, Projection 2050 | From UN 2025 |

The Excel file also contains the full UN 2025 top-200 list.

## Sources
- **Video figures:** 2026 metro estimates as listed by World Population Review (UN World Urbanization Prospects, 2018 revision).
- **New figures:** UN DESA, *World Urbanization Prospects 2025*.

## Notes and corrections
- **New York, Los Angeles, Chicago:** the cards used city-proper populations, not metro areas. The UN 2025 column gives the metro-scale figure (New York ≈13.9 million).
- **Luoyang:** the card shows the same figure as Taipei (2,792,490); unconfirmed.
- 41 cities from the video were not found in the UN 2025 top 200 (most dropped out under the new boundaries; check the full UN list in the Excel file).
