# Best-Selling Albums of All Time, One per Artist

**Video:** [102 Best-Selling Albums of All Time — Ranked by Copies Sold](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vuBWhvU9bAI), published 3 September 2026
**Rows:** 112 albums, one per artist, ranked by the copies shown in the video. Rank 1 is *Thriller*; the video counts down from rank 112

> **This is a selection, not the world's top 112.** The video picks one famous album per artist, including stars from Germany, France, Japan, Korea, Italy and Brazil. The lower entries sold far less than many albums that are not included.

## What's inside
| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Artist, Album, Release Year, Copies Sold (video) | As shown in the video, in millions |
| Published Claim | The worldwide sales **claim** published on Wikipedia: from its *List of best-selling albums* where the album is listed there, otherwise from the album's own article |
| Certified Units | Copies **certified** by industry bodies (RIAA, BPI, IFPI and others). For listed albums this is Wikipedia's total across available markets; for "Certifications only" rows it is the certification we found, mostly in the US |
| Basis | Where the figure comes from |
| Confidence | **Verified**: the card matches the claim on Wikipedia's list. **Partial**: the card is within 10% of a published claim, or matches the album's article or certification. **Unconfirmed**: the card differs by more than 10%, or no figure was found |

**Claimed vs certified:** claims come from labels, artists and the press, and usually run higher than certified units. Certified units only count markets that certify sales.

## By the numbers
- **25 albums show 30 million or more** on their cards; 53 show 20 million or more; 25 show under 10 million. The median card is 17 million.
- **The 1990s lead with 37 albums**, ahead of the 1980s (27), the 2000s and later (23), the 1970s (21) and the years before 1970 (4).
- **Oldest:** *Elvis' Christmas Album* (1957). **Newest:** BTS, *Map of the Soul: 7* (2020).
- **50 of the 112 albums** appear on Wikipedia's *List of best-selling albums*, which requires a reliable claim of at least 20 million and at least 10 million certified units.
- **Confidence:** 34 Verified, 41 Partial, 37 Unconfirmed.

## Sources
Wikipedia's *List of best-selling albums* (claimed and certified figures) and each album's Wikipedia article, which cite IFPI, RIAA, Billboard and national certification bodies. Checked by Global Stat Tide, November 2026.

## Corrections
Where the card differs from the published figure by more than 10%:
- **Eminem, *The Marshall Mathers LP*:** the card shows 32M; the published claim is **25M**.
- **Backstreet Boys, *Millennium*:** the card shows 30M; the published claim is **24M**.
- **Journey, *Greatest Hits*:** the card shows 15M; the published claim is **25M**.
- **Linkin Park, *Hybrid Theory*:** the card shows 20M; the published claim is **25M**.
- **Amy Winehouse, *Back to Black*:** the card shows 16M; the published claim is **20M**. **Boston, *Boston*:** 17M on the card, **20M** published.
- **Hikaru Utada, *First Love*:** the card shows 10M; the album article gives **18M** worldwide, about 8.7M of them in Japan.
- **The Doors, *The Doors*:** the card shows 5M; the album article gives **over 13M**.
- **Tina Turner, *Private Dancer*:** the card shows 20M; the album article gives **over 12M**. **Dido, *No Angel*:** 21M on the card, **15M** published.
- **Iron Butterfly, *In-A-Gadda-Da-Vida*:** the card's 4M matches the US certification (4x Platinum), while Wikipedia's article on the title track says the album has sold over 30M.
- **Whitney Houston, *The Bodyguard*:** the card shows 44M and appears after Pink Floyd's *The Dark Side of the Moon* (45M). Wikipedia gives 45M for both.
- The Note column lists every other difference.

## Notes
- The video title and intro say 102; the video shows **112** album cards.
- **14 albums have certifications only** (no published worldwide total), and for 3 no sales figure was found: Nena, *99 Luftballons*; Marvin Gaye, *What's Going On* (first-year sales only); Roberto Carlos, *20 Grandes Sucessos* (no album of this title found).
- *Saturday Night Fever* and *The Bodyguard* are soundtracks credited to the Bee Gees and Whitney Houston together with various artists.
