# 100 Hits of the 1990s, Ranked by Weeks at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100

**Video:** [Can You Name the 90s Song That Ruled Billboard the Longest?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ml2zqrnV14c), published 4 October 2026
**Rows:** 100 songs, ranked 1 to 100. The video counts down from No. 100 to No. 1

## What's inside
| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rank, Artist, Song, Year Released, Country | As shown in the video. Year Released is the year the song was first released; Country is the act's home country (for groups, where they formed) |
| Weeks at No. 1 (Billboard Hot 100) | Total weeks at the top of the US Hot 100. 0 if the song never reached No. 1 |
| Hot 100 Peak | Highest US Hot 100 position, or "Did not chart" |
| Album | The album or soundtrack the song is best known from. Added for this dataset |
| Confidence | **Verified**: chart figures confirmed by two sources |
| Note | Context for the figures, including airplay charts and chart-rule exceptions |

**How the ranking works:** most weeks at No. 1 first (songs with the same number of weeks keep the order used in the video). Songs that never reached No. 1 follow, ranked by their Hot 100 peak. Songs that never entered the Hot 100 come last. This is a selection of 100 of the decade's biggest hits, not a complete list of every 1990s No. 1.

## By the numbers
- **61 songs reached No. 1**, 30 peaked between No. 2 and No. 42, and 9 never entered the Hot 100 at all.
- **Mariah Carey** has 7 songs on the list (6 solo, 1 with Boyz II Men) and 64 weeks at No. 1 between them, including the top two spots.
- **Where the acts are from:** United States 57 · United Kingdom 13 · Canada 6 · Sweden 5 · Italy 5 · Germany 4 · Spain, Ireland, Australia and Denmark 2 each · Puerto Rico and Jamaica 1 each.
- **Busiest year:** 1996, with 14 songs.

## Sources
Billboard Hot 100 chart records, via Joel Whitburn's Billboard chart references and Billboard itself, cross-checked against the chart tables on each song's Wikipedia page in November 2026. Airplay figures come from Billboard's Hot 100 Airplay chart. This dataset contains chart facts only, no song lyrics.

## Corrections
- **Whigfield, "Saturday Night":** the card shows 1994. The song was first released in Italy in **1992**; it topped the UK chart in 1994.

## Notes
- **All I Want for Christmas Is You (No. 1):** recorded in 1994, but every one of its 22 weeks at No. 1 came between December 2019 and January 2026. In the 1990s there was no commercial single, so it could not chart.
- **Smooth:** 10 of its 12 weeks at No. 1 fell in 1999 and the last 2 in 2000.
- **Why some huge hits never charted:** until December 1998 a song needed a commercial single on sale in the US to enter the Hot 100. "Killing Me Softly", "Don't Speak", "Lovefool" and "Zombie" were radio-only in America. "Iris" (No. 9) and "Torn" (No. 42) entered only after the rule changed.
- **Full titles:** the cards use the short titles "Falling in Love with You" (UB40) and "Everything I Do" (Bryan Adams). The full titles are "(I Can't Help) Falling in Love with You" and "(Everything I Do) I Do It for You".
- Songs by artists excluded under the channel's content policy were removed before the video was made, so a few 1990s No. 1s do not appear.
