## How to read it
- Rates are the **official annual average**: how many units of local currency one US dollar buys.
- **Change %** = (2025 rate − 2000 rate) ÷ 2000 rate × 100. A **positive** number means the currency **lost** value against the dollar.
- For example, Argentina went from **1 peso per dollar in 2000 to about 1,241 in 2025**. That is a change of about **+124,000%**.
- The **World Bank Official Rate (latest)** column is an independent cross-check from the World Bank series, for 2024.

## Sources

## Notes
- Every country uses the **same definition**: the official rate. Parallel-market or black-market rates are not mixed in, so the countries stay comparable.
- **Turkey, Ghana, Belarus:** their currencies were redenominated. All rates are shown in today's currency units, so the change is real.
- **Lebanon:** the official rate moved from 1,507.5 to 89,500 per dollar in 2023–2024, after decades of a fixed peg.
- **Argentina:** the peso was pegged 1:1 to the dollar until early 2002.
- Countries that use the US dollar, or have no official rate for 2000 or 2025, are not ranked.
- This dataset compares exchange rates only. It does not measure inflation, purchasing power or economic health.
